# Escenas de vínculo · B

## saul y dario · The Veyra study, late
*b_saul_dario · nivel B*

- **narración**: They came to sign a paper for their houses. It was signed an hour ago. Neither has left.
- **dario**: Your father has the best brandy in Valcenda and the worst taste in paintings.
- **saul** (alegre): My mother chose the paintings. I chose the brandy.
- **dario**: Then you're the one with the taste.
- **narración**: Saúl takes off his gloves and lays them on the desk. Dario looks at him as though he's done something indecent.
- **dario** (ruborizada): Captain. I've never seen your hands.
- **saul**: I take them off with people I don't have to negotiate with.
- **dario**: You and I negotiated a loan.
- **saul**: And halfway through, you lost your composure. I never knew why. It wasn't the figures.
- **dario**: No. It wasn't the figures.
- **narración**: The study clock strikes the hour. Neither looks at it.
- **saul**: Call me Saúl. In here, at least.
- **dario** (alegre): Saúl. It sounds worse than “Captain.” I like it better.

> Saúl and Dario use first names in private now. In public they're still “Captain” and “Mr. Miravés.”

## irene y isolda · The Archive reading room, after closing
*b_irene_isolda · nivel B*

- **narración**: Irene has let Isolda in through the clerks' door.
- **isolda** (ruborizada): Is this allowed?
- **irene**: No. That's why it's the only hour you can read in peace.
- **narración**: Isolda leafs through a register. Irene watches her, not the register.
- **isolda**: I have to confess something. My grandmother's papers… I didn't need them. My mother had them at home.
- **irene**: I know. I worked it out in the second week. I kept looking anyway.
- **isolda** (ruborizada): Why?
- **irene**: Because you kept coming.
- **narración**: Isolda sets the register down. For the first time all season, her hands are still.
- **isolda**: Can I stop being so formal with you? It feels so far away.
- **irene** (alegre): You can. I've been doing it in my head for weeks.
- **isolda** (alegre): Then… thank you, Irene. For spending three weeks looking for something that wasn't lost.
- **irene**: You're welcome, Isolda. I'd do it again.
- **narración** [si `marca(pista_taller_arcos)`]: As they part, Isolda notices a pair of clerk's sleeves on Irene's desk, stained with ink. She says nothing.

> Isolda calls Irene by her name now. She says it very softly, like someone slipping something into a pocket.

## nilo y camila · The Greda road, mid-afternoon
*b_nilo_camila · nivel B*

- **narración**: Camila's carriage has lost a wheel. Nilo, who happened to be riding by, already has his coat off.
- **camila**: Mr. Lobreda. Do you always turn up when something breaks?
- **nilo** (alegre): Nearly always. It's a family curse. Hold this, please.
- **narración**: Camila holds the wheel without thinking. Then she laughs at herself.
- **camila**: Two years ago, on this very road, a gentleman mended my axle and rode off without asking my name. I've thought of him for two years.
- **nilo** (ruborizada): A gentleman on a piebald horse, with a dreadful hat?
- **camila**: …Yes.
- **nilo**: The hat was my father's.
- **narración**: Camila stands still with the wheel in her hands. Nilo doesn't know where to look.
- **camila** (alegre): The most romantic thing that's happened to me in two years, and it was you, Nilo. How disappointing. And what a relief.
- **nilo** (alegre): “Nilo”? Not “Mr. Lobreda”?
- **camila**: I've held a wheel for you. There's no going back now.

> Camila knows now who mended her carriage two years ago. She hasn't told a soul, but she smiles whenever she sees an ugly hat.

## teo y celia · The empty ballroom, as the musicians pack up
*b_teo_celia · nivel B*

- **narración**: Only one violinist remains, tuning.
- **teo**: Miss Lobreda. You said one has to start somewhere.
- **celia**: I did. And?
- **teo** (ruborizada): Then I'm starting. Will you grant me a dance nobody counts? Not even you.
- **celia** (molesta): I don't know how to dance without counting.
- **teo**: And I don't know how to dance counting. Between us, we make one whole partner.
- **narración**: They dance badly. Teo treads on her once. Celia laughs before he does.
- **celia** (alegre): You laughed late, Teo.
- **teo**: You called me Teo.
- **celia** (ruborizada): It slipped out. You dance so badly there's no way to stay formal.
- **celia**: That was the worst dance of my life.
- **teo**: And?
- **celia** (alegre): And the first one I didn't count.

> Teo and Celia danced in an empty ballroom. The violinist says it was a beautiful disaster.

## dario y celia · The Academy, one morning, at rehearsal
*b_dario_celia · nivel B*

- **narración**: They're rehearsing for the gala. Celia stumbles on a turn and falls to her knees.
- **narración**: Before anyone can laugh, Dario stumbles too, far more noisily, and lies flat on the floor.
- **dario** (alegre): This floor was waxed by an enemy of House Miravés!
- **narración**: The room laughs at him. Nobody looks at Celia.
- **celia** (ruborizada): You did that on purpose.
- **dario**: I never stumble on purpose. I stumble with talent.
- **celia**: Why?
- **dario**: Because it's easier if they laugh at me. I'm used to it. You aren't, Celia.
- **celia**: You called me Celia.
- **dario**: I've just fallen over in front of twenty people for you. I think I've earned it.
- **celia** (alegre): You've earned it, Dario. But next time, let me fall. I want to learn to get up on my own.
- **dario**: Done. And I want to learn what it feels like not to pretend.

> Dario fell at rehearsal so that nobody would see Celia fall. She's asked him not to do it again.

## teo y irene · The Salvats' library
*b_teo_irene · nivel B*

- **narración**: Teo has come to return a book nobody ever lent him.
- **irene**: That book isn't mine.
- **teo**: I know. It's an excuse with a binding.
- **irene**: It's a poor one.
- **teo** (alegre): It's the best I've got. I spend the good ones in the salons.
- **narración**: Teo takes out a small notebook and sets it on the table, unopened.
- **teo**: I write verse. For a lady who never believes it. I'm not going to read them to you. I just wanted someone to know they exist.
- **irene**: Why me?
- **teo**: Because you don't laugh when it isn't funny. And this isn't.
- **irene**: Teo. If you wrote them without reaching for a laugh, read them. I'll tell you whether I believe them.
- **teo** (ruborizada): You called me Teo.
- **irene** (alegre): It's your name. It's accurate.

> Teo showed Irene a notebook he shows no one. She hasn't told him whether she believed the verses.

## saul y isolda · The same balcony as the first time
*b_saul_isolda · nivel B*

- **narración**: This time it's Isolda who has come out to find him.
- **isolda**: My father has written to yours. He says the convoy alliance needs more than signatures.
- **saul**: I know. My father told me in the very same words. I think they copy each other's letters.
- **isolda** (ruborizada): You're very kind. But I could never love you the way they expect.
- **saul**: Nor I you, Miss Marén.
- **narración**: They look at each other. Neither asks why. Both understand.
- **saul** (alegre): That's the most honest thing anyone has said to me in this city.
- **isolda**: And what if… what if it suited us both for no one ever to ask?
- **saul**: Then we'd be the two most honest liars in Valcenda.
- **isolda**: May I call you Saúl? If we're going to lie together, we might as well use first names.
- **saul**: Isolda. We haven't decided anything yet.
- **isolda**: No. But I'm not afraid any more of it being decided for me.

> Saúl and Isolda talked about convoys, their fathers and what isn't asked. They left the balcony sharing a secret.

## celia y camila · Celia's bedroom, at midnight
*b_celia_camila · nivel B*

- **narración**: Camila comes in to put out the candle and finds Celia writing in a notebook.
- **camila**: Love letters, at this hour?
- **celia** (molesta): A list.
- **camila**: Of suitors?
- **celia**: Of everyone who looked down on me in a ballroom. Since I was fifteen.
- **camila**: How many so far?
- **celia** (ruborizada): Forty-three.
- **camila** (alegre): Put down House Miravés. They're claiming a ship from me that's at the bottom of the sea. Help me hate them, and I'll help you burn the notebook.
- **celia**: Is it true, about the Clara de Nácar?
- **camila**: What the Sheet says is usually true, Celia. The trouble is what it leaves out.
- **celia**: Mrs. Ferrán… may I call you Camila?
- **camila** (alegre): You've been doing it for three weeks whenever you think I'm asleep.

> Celia and Camila talked until late. Celia's list has a new name on it, and it isn't anyone who looked down on her.

## dario y isolda · The Marén office, beside the model of the harbour
*b_dario_isolda · nivel B*

- **narración**: Dario looks at the model of Nácar harbour the way he did as a boy.
- **dario**: This is the jetty where I used to hide from my aunt.
- **isolda**: And I hid there with you. I never knew what you were running from.
- **dario**: Dinners. My aunt dines very late, with very pale people.
- **isolda**: That's no reason to hide.
- **dario**: At eight, it was.
- **narración**: Isolda rests a hand on his sleeve, without trembling. It's the first time she's been the one to do it.
- **isolda**: Dario. You stood up for me when I couldn't speak. I can a little now. If you ever need someone to speak for you…
- **dario** (ruborizada): Just “Dario”, Isolda Marén?
- **isolda** (alegre): I called you Dario when we were eight. I've only taken it back.
- **dario**: My aunt wants something from this season. I don't know what. If I find out, you'll be the first to know.
- **isolda** [si `marca(pujador_miraves)`]: Does it have to do with a chest of burned papers?
- **dario** [si `marca(pujador_miraves)`]: …You'll be the second. It seems you're already the first.

> Isolda has offered to speak for Dario if he ever needs it. He didn't say no.

## nilo y celia · The Lobreda kitchen, late
*b_nilo_celia · nivel B*

- **narración**: Celia finds Nilo staring at the calendar by the pantry.
- **celia**: The 20th.
- **nilo**: The 20th.
- **celia**: There's the gala at the theatre. The Matchmaker will want to send us both.
- **nilo**: I know.
- **celia**: If she asks, I'll say you have an assignation. With a very plain lady, so nobody asks who.
- **nilo** (alegre): Are you helping me?
- **celia**: Once. You let me do it on my own. I'm returning the favour.
- **narración**: Nilo turns the calendar to face the wall. His hands are shaking slightly, and Celia pretends not to see.
- **nilo**: Celia… does anyone else know?
- **celia**: Only family. And family doesn't talk.

> Nilo and Celia talked in the kitchen. Celia says her cousin has “an engagement” on the night of the 20th. She can't lie, but she's trying.
