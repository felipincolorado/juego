# Semana 4 · Full Moon

**Full moon.** On the night of Saturday the 20th the moon is full. The Bridge Theatre opens its glass roof for the gala performance, as it does every autumn, and all of Valcenda will want to be beneath it.

## Apertura

- **nota**: Madam: the second Sheet has frightened more people than the first. Three houses have asked to see their bundles at the Archive. The Archive says they're being restored.
- **nota**: And an invitation has arrived that I didn't expect: Amalia Miravés will receive you for tea. At sunset.
- si `marca(reina_interes) o marca(reina_conmovida)`: Mireya says the Queen has asked after you twice this week. Once, by name.
- si `marca(vistos_en_arcos)`: Someone slipped an unsigned note under your door: “Boats shouldn't go near Arcos Bajos at night. For your sake and for others'.” The handwriting is small and very neat.
- si `marca(mara_investiga)`: Mara Calce has put up notices on the Street of Seals: ten crowns for the Sheet's printer. Six people already claim to know. None of them agree.
- si `marca(lavanda_hablada)`: The Veyras and the Maréns have dined together. Saúl and Isolda sat side by side and talked about books all evening. Their fathers seemed delighted. They seemed at peace.
- Opción **Go to Amalia Miravés's tea** → Every curtain in the Miravés house is drawn. Amalia is pale, gracious and very exact. “Old papers hurt people who don't deserve it,” she tells you. “I only want no one to read them before their time. Not even my nephew.” As you leave, you notice she hasn't touched her cup.
- Opción **Send a note of apology** → Amalia replies by return: “I understand. I don't care to go out by day either.” You can't tell whether it's a joke.

## Peticiones

- **nilo** pide no ir a nada el día 20: Nilo: “Would you do me a favour? Don't send me anywhere on Saturday. Don't ask me why. I owe you— I'll owe you one.”
  - Si se cumple: On Sunday, Nilo brings you flowers from the Greda road. He has shadows under his eyes and an enormous smile. “Thank you for not asking.”
  - Si no: Nilo doesn't protest. It's the worst thing he could have done.
- **isolda** pide `s4_teatro`: Isolda, eyes on the floor: “If Irene is going to the theatre… could I go too? I've never seen the moon from there.”
  - Si se cumple: Isolda comes back from the theatre without a word. But that night, at the residence, you hear her singing softly.
  - Si no: Isolda says the moon looks the same from the window. And it's true, almost.

## Supper among the presses · día 16 · imprenta
*cena · plazas 2–3 · cualquiera*

> Mara Calce invites guests to sup at the printing house, among freshly printed sheets. She promises an after-dinner game: guessing who writes the Sheet.

Requisito: chisme 5, intelecto 5, presencia 5
- \+ Mara knows more gossip than anyone and gives it away for free
- − Anything said may be in print by Monday

**Carta**

*Dear rival,*

You put couples together and I put news together: we're more alike than you'd care to admit. On Tuesday I'm dining at the printing house, among the presses, which is where one thinks best. After dessert we'll play at guessing who writes that Sheet that's stealing my readers. Whoever guesses right dines free all autumn.

*Mara Calce*

**Incidente (día 16)** · Mara hands out slips: each guest must write a name. She reads her own aloud: “Dario Miravés. No one sees him by day, his carriage never opens its curtains and his aunt buys burned papers.” The table turns to {a}.

- **Play along and hear every suspicion** (chisme, d 5)
  - Éxito: {mejor} plays gracefully and listens. Six names come out. Dario's twice, Mara's once, and once, very quietly, “someone from the Archive.”
  - A medias: The game is fun, but everyone repeats Dario's name, as if it were a chorus.
  - Fallo: {mejor} writes a name just to play along, and Mara reads it aloud. It was the wrong one.
- **Dismantle the suspicion: whoever writes knows archives, not carriages** (intelecto, d 5)
  - Éxito: {mejor} reasons aloud: the Sheet cites exact folios, uses discard paper and appears the day after visits to the Archive. “It's an archivist, or a clerk.” Mara writes something down. The table falls to thinking.
  - A medias: The reasoning is sound, but Mara cuts it off with a toast. Nobody knows whether she heard it.
  - Fallo: {mejor} gets muddled over the folios and Mara laughs: “That's just what the author would say.”
- **Decline gracefully: one doesn't play with other people's names** (presencia, d 5)
  - Éxito: {mejor} tears up the slip without writing anything. “This table has enough names in print already.” Two guests do the same. Mara, to everyone's surprise, does too.
  - A medias: No one else declines, but no one presses the point either.
  - Fallo: The refusal sounds as if {mejor} has something to hide. Mara notes it in her book.

**Desenlace · favorable** — A long table
- publico: {grupo} supped among the presses at the Calce printing house. It was the most talked-about dinner of the week, and Mara won't print half of it.
- privado: Mara sends you the slips from the game in an envelope. “So you can see what Valcenda thinks. It no longer interests me: I know it wasn't me.”
  - si `asiste(irene)`: Irene arrived late, her fingers stained with thick printer's ink. She said she'd been restoring a bundle. Nobody at the Calce printing house restores bundles with that ink.
  - si `asiste(dario)`: Dario heard his name in the game and laughed along. In the carriage home, he opened the curtains for the first time.

**Desenlace · medias** — Ink and wine
- publico: {grupo} supped with Mara Calce. The wine was good and the talk was too much.
- privado: In the morning, the Chronicle prints nothing about the supper. Mara is keeping it. That worries you more than if she'd published it.
  - si `asiste(irene)`: Irene kept her hands under the table. When she raised them for a toast, her fingers were black with ink.

**Desenlace · desaire** — Names read aloud
- publico: At the Calce supper, names were read aloud that shouldn't have been. Several houses have stopped greeting {grupo}.
- privado: Dario doesn't write to you this week. It's the first time his charm has failed you.

## An archery party · día 18 · jardines
*paseo · plazas 2–4 · cualquiera*

> Leonora hosts an archery contest among the asters. The aim is poor and the conversation good.

Requisito: presencia 5, estatus 4, baile 4
- \+ A good shot is remembered longer than a good waltz
- − Creditors of several houses are here: the reckonings aren't over

**Carta**

*Dear Matchmaker,*

On Thursday, bows and targets among the asters. I can't promise good aim, but I can promise good company and a silver prize for whoever hits the centre. I've forgiven the auction: do come, and bring those who know how to lose gracefully.

*Leonora Serravalle*

**Incidente (día 18)** · Between shots, a moneylender's clerk approaches Teo and reminds him, loudly, of a debt. Teo cracks a joke and nobody laughs. {a} is watching him.

- **Take Teo aside and talk to him properly** (presencia, d 5)
  - Éxito: Under the chestnuts, Teo stops joking. “My father wants to marry me to money. And I want someone to laugh with me, not at me.” He says it once and doesn't repeat it.
  - A medias: Teo starts to speak seriously and, halfway through, makes a joke. It's slipped away from him again.
  - Fallo: Teo laughs at the offer before hearing it. “Talk? If I wanted to talk, I'd have become a priest.”
- **Dismiss the clerk with authority** (estatus, d 5)
  - Éxito: {mejor} reminds the clerk he's a guest in someone else's house. He leaves with a bow. Teo says thank you with his eyes.
  - A medias: The clerk steps back, but waits by the gate.
  - Fallo: The clerk replies that the debt is public. Now it is.
- **Help Teo turn it into a joke** (chisme, d 4)
  - Éxito: Between them, {mejor} and Teo turn the debt into the best story of the afternoon. Leonora weeps with laughter. When it's over, Teo goes very quiet.
  - A medias: The joke half works. Some laugh; others look at the clerk.
  - Fallo: The joke falls into a silence you can hear from the house.

**Desenlace · favorable** — Dead centre
- publico: {grupo} shone at the Serravalle archery. The silver prize went home with the Matchmaker's party, and Leonora doesn't hold it against them.
  - si `asiste(celia) y asiste(dario)`: Celia Lobreda hit the centre at her first attempt. Dario Miravés deliberately missed his next three shots so no one could compare them.
  - si `asiste(saul)`: Captain Veyra shot as he had in the army: without grace and without missing. He took off his gloves for the final shot. People remarked on it.
- privado: As you leave, Leonora says: “That Rovena boy is better than he pretends.” She doesn't say which side of him surprised her.
  - si `asiste(teo) y opcion(s4_arco) == hablar`: Teo writes to you that night, without a single joke: “Thank you for the conversation. I don't know if I'll have another, but I can no longer say I never had one.”

**Desenlace · medias** — Arrows in the lawn
- publico: The Serravalle archery party was pleasant. Most of the arrows ended up in the lawn.
- privado: The moneylender's clerk left with a message for House Rovena. Teo knows and says nothing.

**Desenlace · desaire** — Debts in public
- publico: At the Serravalle Gardens there was more talk of debts than of arrows. {grupo} left before the prize.
- privado: Teo doesn't show his face in any salon until Sunday. Nilo says he's fine. Nilo always says everything's fine.

## A private visit · día 19 · residencia
*visita · plazas 2–2 · cualquiera*

> An afternoon without salons or Chronicle. Two people already on first-name terms, a room with a fire and nobody watching.

- \+ +2 closeness. Sets up the threshold scene (A)
- − No reputation: nobody finds out

**Al completarse**
- publico: A private afternoon. No one in Valcenda will know.
- privado: {a} and {b} spent the afternoon at the residence. Bruna brought tea twice and, the second time, didn't dare interrupt.
  - si `asiste(saul) y asiste(dario)`: Saúl and Dario played chess without finishing the game. Saúl left his gloves on the hall table and went home without them.
  - si `asiste(irene) y asiste(isolda)`: Irene read aloud and Isolda listened with her eyes closed. Then Isolda read, and didn't stumble once.
  - si `asiste(nilo) y asiste(camila)`: Camila taught Nilo to play cards the way widows play: without mercy. Nilo lost everything and laughed the whole time.
  - si `asiste(teo) y asiste(celia)`: Teo tried to teach Celia how to tell a joke. Celia rehearsed the delivery nine times. On the tenth, Teo genuinely laughed.
  - si `asiste(dario) y asiste(celia)`: Dario and Celia danced without music in the small parlour. She didn't count. He didn't pretend. Bruna says she didn't see them.
  - si `asiste(teo) y asiste(irene)`: Teo finally read one of his verses. Irene didn't say whether she believed it, but she asked for another.
  - si `asiste(saul) y asiste(isolda)`: Saúl and Isolda drafted a letter to their fathers about the convoys together. It took three hours and they laughed a great deal. They didn't send it.

## The gala performance · día 20 · teatro
*teatro · plazas 4–4 · parejas · OBLIGATORIO*

> A gala performance with the glass roof opened to the full moon. You have a box: two couples, in full view of the house.

Requisito: intelecto 5, estatus 5, presencia 5
- \+ All Valcenda will be watching the boxes, not the stage
- − The performance ends late, with the moon high
- − Dario's aunt has the next box

**Carta**

*To the Matchmaker of the Season,*

The Bridge Theatre has the honour of reserving the fourth box for you at Saturday's gala: “The Lady of the Lighthouse,” in three acts, with the roof open to the full moon. We ask that two couples occupy it. An empty box on such a night is talked about more than any scene.

*The management of the Bridge Theatre*

**Incidente (día 20)** · In the interval, Amalia Miravés takes the next box with its curtains drawn. Half the house looks over, then at {a}. Someone whispers, “The Sheet was right.”

- **Greet Amalia in public, with every courtesy** (estatus, d 5)
  - Éxito: {mejor} crosses to the next box and greets her. Amalia draws back a curtain, a hand's breadth, and smiles. The house decides there's nothing to see.
  - A medias: Amalia returns the greeting without opening the curtains. The house keeps looking, if less.
  - Fallo: The greeting comes too late and looks like an apology. Now the house is watching both boxes.
- **Make the box shine brighter than the gossip** (presencia, d 5)
  - Éxito: The fourth box laughs, chats and applauds out of turn so charmingly that the house stops looking at the curtains.
  - A medias: It works until the third act, when someone points at Amalia's box again.
  - Fallo: The laughter from the box sounds forced under the moon. Someone calls for silence.
- **Notice who isn't where they should be tonight** (intelecto, d 5)
  - Éxito: {mejor} counts the boxes: Amalia is here, Dario is here, Mara is here. Two people are missing: a gentleman who's always first to arrive, and Irene Salvat, who said she'd come.
  - A medias: {mejor} senses someone is missing, but can't tell who before the lights go down.
  - Fallo: {mejor} watches the boxes so closely that the end of the play goes unseen. Half the theatre remarks on it.

**Incidente de riesgo (si `asiste(nilo)`, sustituye al anterior)** · As the glass roof opens, the full moon's light pours into the house. Nilo goes very pale, excuses himself and leaves the box without looking at anyone. {a} watches him head toward the river.

- **Cover for him: invent an excuse for the box** (chisme, d 5)
  - Éxito: “An assignation,” says {mejor}, “with a lady who doesn't wish to be seen.” The box laughs, so does the house, and nobody asks again.
  - A medias: The excuse half convinces. Someone in the box opposite notes the time.
  - Fallo: The excuse contradicts itself. By the third act, half the house knows Nilo Lobreda ran out.
- **Follow him, in case he needs help** (presencia, d 5)
  - Éxito: {mejor} catches up with him by the river. Nilo is shaking, his back turned, and asks without turning round to be left alone. “Please. I'll tell you everything tomorrow. Not tonight.” {mejor} goes. And keeps that promise.
  - A medias: {mejor} loses him among the lanterns on the bridge. All that's left is his coat, carefully folded over the railing.
  - Fallo: {mejor} follows him to the river, and someone else follows them both. By morning it's going round that Nilo Lobreda “fled the theatre like a frightened animal.”
- **Let him go and give out that he's indisposed** (estatus, d 4)
  - Éxito: {mejor} sends a note to the management: Mr. Lobreda is indisposed. The box is one short, but dignified.
  - A medias: The note arrives, but the empty seat is visible from the whole house.
  - Fallo: The note goes astray. For two acts, Nilo's seat is the most-watched thing in the theatre.

**Desenlace · favorable** — Under the glass roof
- publico: The fourth box was the most admired at the gala. {grupo} watched “The Lady of the Lighthouse” under the moon, and the house watched them.
  - si `asiste(irene) y asiste(isolda)`: Isolda Marén and Irene Salvat shared a programme for the whole performance. In the third act, Isolda stopped reading it and looked at Irene.
  - si `asiste(saul) y asiste(dario)`: Captain Veyra and Mr. Miravés shared the box and didn't exchange a word in public. In private, they say, they didn't stop.
  - si `asiste(celia) y marca(celia_error_tapado)`: Celia Lobreda tripped on the box stairs, got up on her own and bowed to the house. The ovation was hers.
- privado: The Queen wasn't there, but she sent Mireya. Mireya tells you: “Her Majesty will want to know who was missing tonight. I already know.”
  - si `asiste(nilo) y marca(nilo_cubierto)`: At dawn, Nilo leaves a note at the residence: “Thank you. Some day I'll explain. I promise you.” This time he hasn't corrected himself.

**Desenlace · medias** — A box half watched
- publico: The gala was beautiful and the fourth box respectable. The house spent more time watching the Miravés curtains.
- privado: At the exit, someone has left a folded sheet in every carriage. It's the third Sheet, a day early.

**Desenlace · desaire** — The night of the moon
- publico: At the Bridge Theatre gala, the Matchmaker's box caused more talk than the play. None of what's being said is good.
- privado: Mireya writes you a single line: “The Queen will read the Sheet before the Chronicle.”

**Desenlace · sin_asistentes**
- publico: The fourth box stood empty on gala night. Under the full moon, the whole house could see it.

## The Unsigned Sheet · nº 3

*Someone in the salons is not what they seem.*

They arrive as the sun sets. Their carriage never opens its curtains. Their family buys burned papers. Interesting. Why has no one ever seen them at noon?
- si `marca(nilo_visto) y no marca(nilo_cubierto)`: Nor are they the only one. Another gentleman leaves theatres when the moon rises. The Threshold Pact had a page for each of them.

## Sólo para sus ojos

- si `asistio(s4_cena_calce, irene)`: Irene had printer's ink on her fingers on Tuesday. Not ledger ink: printer's ink. You know the difference; Mara taught you.
- si `marca(pista_ausencias)`: On theatre night two people were missing: Nilo, who is always first to arrive, and Irene, who had said she'd come. The Sheet was in the carriages before the performance ended.
- si `marca(pista_archivera)`: You said it aloud at Mara's: whoever writes it is an archivist, or a clerk. You haven't been able to stop thinking about it since.
- si `marca(dario_sospechoso)`: Half of Valcenda suspects Dario. You don't. Dario can't write a letter without spelling mistakes, and the Sheet has none.
- si `marca(nilo_visto)`: Nilo was shaking by the river and wouldn't turn round. He promised to tell you everything. You decide not to ask until he's ready.
- si `marca(amalia_conocida)`: Amalia Miravés wants no one to read those papers “before their time.” You begin to understand she doesn't want them to do harm, but to keep harm from someone.
- si `marca(teo_conversacion_seria)`: Teo wants someone who laughs with him, not at him. He said it once. That's enough for you.
- si `semana == 4`: Two weeks left. The couples are no longer possibilities: they are people waiting for each other in corners.
