# Semana 5 · The Reckoning

**The Nácar storms.** The Nácar storms arrive, and with them the southern convoys. Before the Grand Ball, the houses settle their debts: those that can be paid and those that can't.

## Apertura

- **nota**: Madam: Captain Veyra has finally opened the letter from Tiria. It's an order: he must return to the army after the Grand Ball.
- **nota**: Unless he seals an engagement at the Archive before then. In that case he may stay and serve here, “for the alliance of his house.”
- **nota**: And Mara Calce is requesting an interview. In writing this time, and sealed. She says it's the last time she'll ask.
- si `marca(nilo_cubierto) o marca(nilo_confia)`: Nilo has come to breakfast twice this week. He hasn't told you anything. But he stays longer, like someone about to say something.
- si `marca(nilo_visto) y no marca(nilo_cubierto)`: The salons are talking about Nilo Lobreda “and his escapes.” He smiles as he always does. He no longer sleeps as he used to.
- si `marca(dario_sospechoso)`: Someone threw stones at the Miravés carriage on the bridge. Dario says it was nothing. He had the curtains open.
- si `marca(teo_bufon)`: Teo is the most sought-after guest of the week. People invite him to make them laugh. Nobody invites him for anything else.
- si `marca(isolda_hablo_primero)`: Isolda said good morning to you aloud, from the doorway, before you'd even seen her arrive. Bruna nearly dropped the tray.
- Opción **Grant Mara the interview** → Mara doesn't ask about couples. She asks whether you write the Sheet. You laugh. She doesn't. “Someone who knows everything about your eight… If it isn't you, it's someone very close to you.” She leaves satisfied, without having written a single line.
- Opción **Refuse, firmly** → Mara returns the broken seal with a note: “Then I'll write what I think, which is worse than anything you'd have said.”

## Peticiones

- **nilo** pide no ir a nada el día 25: Nilo: “My house wants me at the reading of the articles on Thursday. And I promised Camila I'd help her with the ship's accounts that same day. Don't send me. Please. This time I want to keep the promise that matters.”
  - Si se cumple: Nilo spends Thursday among the Ferrán account books. His father writes him a furious letter. Nilo reads it, folds it and goes on adding up.
  - Si no: Nilo goes to the reading, as his house wishes. Camila does the accounts alone. When he arrives that night, they're already done.
- **camila** pide `s5_cena_deudas`: Camila: “I want to go to the debts supper myself. If the Miravés are going to read my name aloud, let them do it looking at me.”
  - Si se cumple: Camila comes back from the Serravalle villa and pulls off her gloves at the door, like someone setting down a weight. “Done,” she says. Nothing more.
  - Si no: Camila hears from others what was said about her at the supper. She doesn't reproach you for it. That's worse.

## A voice lesson · día 23 · academia
*clase · plazas 1–3 · cualquiera*

> Inés teaches how to speak in a crowded room without raising your voice. At the Grand Ball, she says, the Queen will only hear those who can make themselves heard.

- \+ +1 Presence for each attendee (up to 4)
- − Inés makes everyone sing. Everyone

**Carta**

*Dear Matchmaker,*

On Tuesday I give the last lesson of the season. Not dancing: voice. Whoever can't make themselves heard at the Grand Ball will dance beautifully and nobody will learn their name. Send me the quiet ones. And those who talk too much, so they learn when to stop.

*Inés*

**Al completarse**
- publico: {grupo} took a voice lesson with Inés. At the Academy, even the porter was heard singing.
- privado: Inés writes: “They can make themselves heard now. What they need is something to say. That I don't teach.”
  - si `asiste(isolda)`: Isolda sang a whole verse alone. When she finished, nobody applauded for a moment, and then everyone did at once.
  - si `asiste(irene)`: Irene refused to sing and read a poem aloud instead. Inés said it counted. Irene read far too well for a first time.
  - si `asiste(saul)`: Saúl sang an army march, perfectly in tune and without a smile. Inés asked for another, slower one. That one he sang smiling.

## The convoys come in · día 24 · muelles
*recepcion · plazas 2–4 · cualquiera*

> The Maréns and the Veyras greet the southern convoys at the wharves, between storms. They want Valcenda to see their alliance.

Requisito: estatus 5, presencia 5, intelecto 4
- \+ Both houses are in excellent spirits
- − The Nácar sky has no respect for receptions
- − The insurers watch every ship as though it were about to sink

**Carta**

*Distinguished Madam,*

On Wednesday the first convoys escorted by land by House Veyra and insured by sea by House Marén reach Valcenda. We shall celebrate their arrival at the wharves, if the storm allows, and even if it doesn't. We would be honoured to see your young people. Above all, those who already know our children.

*Tobías Marén and Ernesto Veyra*

**Incidente (día 24)** · A gust of wind brings down the reception awning. In the confusion, both fathers announce aloud that “the alliance deserves a tie stronger than a contract.” Everyone looks at {a}.

- **Restore order in the rain and save the reception** (presencia, d 5)
  - Éxito: {mejor} organises the servants, the guests and the rain. When the sun comes out again, the fathers toast the alliance and the whole wharf applauds.
  - A medias: The reception is half saved. The toast is made under a crooked awning.
  - Fallo: The rain wins. The guests scatter and the announcement hangs in the air, soaked.
- **Distract the fathers before they name names** (estatus, d 5)
  - Éxito: {mejor} proposes a toast to the boatmen before anyone can say a name. The fathers exchange annoyed looks. Saúl and Isolda, relieved ones.
  - A medias: The toast comes in time, but one of the fathers manages to say “our children” before drinking.
  - Fallo: The toast comes too late: both names have already been said, together, in front of the whole wharf.
- **Notice which ship the insurers check most carefully** (intelecto, d 4)
  - Éxito: The Marén insurers inspect a sister ship of the Clara de Nácar. One mutters: “This one won't sink. That one had no reason to either.” {mejor} hears it.
  - A medias: The insurers check every ship the same. But one crosses himself at the sight of the last.
  - Fallo: {mejor} gets too close to the ship and a sailor rudely tells them to step back.

**Desenlace · favorable** — Ships and toasts
- publico: The Nácar convoys arrived between storms and applause. {grupo} were on the wharf and were the only dry thing all afternoon.
  - si `asiste(saul) y asiste(isolda)`: Captain Veyra and Miss Marén greeted the first ship together. Their fathers wept. The two of them shook hands like business partners.
  - si `asiste(camila)`: Mrs. Ferrán watched the Clara de Nácar's sister ship go by without looking away. Then she asked for a glass and toasted those who never arrived.
- privado: Tobías Marén writes to you: “My daughter has never talked so much at a reception. I don't know what you've done to her, but carry on.”
  - si `asiste(isolda) y marca(isolda_hablo_primero)`: In front of everyone, Isolda asked her father to let her choose for herself. He said yes before thinking about it. Now he is thinking about it.

**Desenlace · medias** — Wet but allied
- publico: The convoy reception ended in the rain. The Marén–Veyra alliance, on the other hand, seems stronger than ever.
- privado: Saúl leaves the wharf without saying goodbye, at a military pace. Isolda watches him go and says nothing.

**Desenlace · desaire** — The storm won
- publico: The storm ruined the convoy reception. Houses Marén and Veyra blame the sky. Some blame others.
- privado: Both fathers write to you the same day, separately, with the same complaint. They do copy each other's letters.

## The reading of the articles · día 25 · archivo
*recepcion · plazas 2–3 · cualquiera*

> The Lobredas and the Veyras read the Greda pass agreements at the Archive. They say they'll only discuss caravans. Nobody believes it.

Requisito: estatus 5, intelecto 5, chisme 4
- \+ Whoever understands the papers understands the season
- − The Veyras already have another alliance on the table

**Carta**

*Madam,*

On Thursday, in the Archive's record room, our houses will renew the Greda pass agreements. The presence of trustworthy witnesses would be appreciated. Miss Celia Lobreda and Captain Saúl Veyra are particularly invited, though nobody has said why.

*Houses Lobreda and Veyra*

**Incidente (día 25)** · On the last page of the articles there's a new clause: “The pass shall be bound by marriage.” It names no one, but there are two empty chairs by the table. {a} reads it first.

- **Read it aloud, exactly as written** (intelecto, d 5)
  - Éxito: {mejor} reads the clause calmly. The fathers nod. Celia goes very still. Saúl looks at the window.
  - A medias: The clause is read, but no one says anything afterwards. The silence lasts a whole minute.
  - Fallo: {mejor} stumbles over the house names. Someone laughs nervously and the meeting sours.
- **Propose an amendment: “if both parties so wish”** (estatus, d 5)
  - Éxito: {mejor} proposes the amendment with such courtesy that nobody dares refuse. “If both parties so wish” is written in fresh ink.
  - A medias: The amendment is accepted “for consideration.” Nobody knows what that means.
  - Fallo: The fathers reject the amendment in unison, as if they'd rehearsed it.
- **Ask the archivist on duty who drafted the clause** (chisme, d 4)
  - Éxito: The archivist on duty is Irene Salvat. She says the clause arrived already written. She has a folded sheet in her apron pocket, and covers it with her hand.
  - A medias: The archivist on duty says she can't discuss private documents. It's Irene Salvat. She won't meet anyone's eye.
  - Fallo: The question is heard across the room. The fathers look at {mejor} as at an intruder.

**Desenlace · favorable** — Fresh ink
- publico: Houses Lobreda and Veyra renewed the pass agreements. {grupo} signed as witnesses in impeccable hands.
  - si `asiste(celia) y asiste(saul)`: Celia Lobreda and Saúl Veyra sat in the two empty chairs. They didn't look at each other. Valcenda has already set them a date.
- privado: Leaving the Archive, Celia's father shakes your hand: “You understand houses.” You can't tell whether it's praise or a threat.
  - si `asiste(celia) y opcion(s5_capitulaciones) == enmendar`: Celia catches up with you on the stairs and says, very fast: “Thank you for ‘if both parties so wish.’ I don't know yet what I wish. But now I'm allowed to find out.”

**Desenlace · medias** — An empty chair
- publico: The reading of the pass articles was long and discreet. Everything was signed except the last page.
- privado: The last page stays at the Archive, unsigned. Someone has filed it in a bundle numbered 84.

**Desenlace · desaire** — Torn papers
- publico: The reading of the Lobreda–Veyra articles ended in raised voices. The pass caravans will keep passing. The houses, for now, won't.
- privado: Celia hasn't left her room all day. Camila says she's fine. Camila hasn't left hers either.

## The debts supper · día 26 · jardines
*cena · plazas 3–3 · cualquiera · OBLIGATORIO*

> On the eve of the reckoning, the great houses dine at the Serravalle villa and settle their debts aloud. It's a tradition. It's a cruelty.

Requisito: intelecto 5, chisme 5, estatus 5
- \+ What's settled tonight won't reach the Grand Ball
- − Tonight the Miravés claim against Mrs. Ferrán falls due
- − Indebted houses don't forgive anyone who watches them pay

**Carta**

*Dear Matchmaker,*

As every autumn, on the eve of the reckoning I gather at the villa the houses that owe one another something. The food is good, the talk is plain, and everyone leaves with their accounts settled or their enemies declared. This time, I fear, there will be both. Bring three of your young people, and let them be good at sums.

*Leonora Serravalle*

**Incidente (día 26)** · Amalia Miravés rises and hands her nephew a paper: “You read it, Dario. It's your claim.” It's the Clara de Nácar debt against the widow Ferrán. The whole table looks at {a}.

- **Prove with the papers that the Marén insurance already paid for that ship** (intelecto, d 5)
  - Éxito: {mejor} recalls the dates: the Marén insurance paid for the Clara de Nácar in full, without a quarrel. Claiming it again is collecting twice. Amalia sits down slowly. The debt falls.
  - A medias: The argument is sound, but papers are missing. The debt is postponed until spring.
  - Fallo: {mejor} gets the year wrong. Amalia smiles, politely. The debt stands.
- **Negotiate on the Matchmaker's behalf: postpone the debt** (estatus, d 5)
  - Éxito: {mejor} proposes postponing the debt until after spring, guaranteed by the Matchmaker. Amalia accepts with a look that says: “You owe me one.”
  - A medias: It's postponed a month. Just enough to get through the Grand Ball.
  - Fallo: The proposal sounds like charity. Camila turns it down aloud, out of pride.
- **Strike back: ask about the chest of burned papers** (chisme, d 5)
  - Éxito: “And the papers from lot 12, Mrs. Miravés?” The table turns to Amalia. Amalia puts the claim away unread. Dario doesn't look up from his plate.
  - A medias: The question unsettles her, but Amalia sidesteps it gracefully. The claim is read regardless.
  - Fallo: The question sounds like slander. Leonora calls for order, and the table sides with the Miravés.

**Desenlace · favorable** — Accounts settled
- publico: The debts supper ended with more accounts settled than enemies made. {grupo} left the Serravalle villa with their heads high.
  - si `asiste(dario) y marca(dario_sin_mascara)`: Dario Miravés refused to read his aunt's claim. “I didn't draft it,” he said, “and I won't sign it.” It's the first time anyone has contradicted Amalia in public.
  - si `asiste(camila)`: Mrs. Ferrán heard her name without blinking and answered with a single sentence: “The sea has already collected on that ship.” Leonora asked for it to be engraved on a plate.
  - si `asiste(teo)`: Aurelio Rovena announced that his son will seek “an advantageous match” before the Grand Ball. Teo toasted it without a single joke.
- privado: Leonora writes to you at midnight: “For twenty years nobody has left this supper happy. That was your doing. Don't do it again: you're ruining my tradition.”
  - si `marca(deuda_saldada) y asiste(isolda)`: That night Isolda learned why her family paid without a quarrel: a Marén underwriter signed off the ship knowing it was rotten. Her father paid to keep it quiet. Isolda didn't cry at the supper. She cried afterwards, alone, and then went to see Camila.

**Desenlace · medias** — Accounts outstanding
- publico: At the debts supper, half was settled and the rest postponed. That's normal. {grupo} made nothing worse, which tonight is saying a lot.
- privado: Camila walks home alone, in the rain. She wouldn't take a carriage.

**Desenlace · desaire** — Enemies declared
- publico: This autumn's debts supper will be remembered for the shouting. Mrs. Ferrán leaves it with the debt standing and half the houses against her.
- privado: Amalia Miravés sends you a bouquet of white flowers. No note. Bruna throws them out without asking.

**Desenlace · sin_asistentes**
- publico: The Matchmaker sent no one to the debts supper. The houses dined without witnesses, and that never ends well.

## The Unsigned Sheet · nº 4

*The next issue will be the last. It will carry names.*

The Archive began to burn at the third hour of the night of the 14th of Nieblas, eighty years ago, in the lineage room. Not the tax room, as the official history says. Someone chose what should burn. The Grand Ball will learn who.
