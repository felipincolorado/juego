# Escenas de vínculo · C

## saul y dario · A terrace, at the end of the evening
*c_saul_dario · nivel C*

- **narración**: Saúl checks his watch. Dario studies the stars. Both pretend not to have noticed the other.
- **dario** (alegre): Captain. You look at the time as if it owed you money.
- **saul**: It does. I promised my father I'd be home before midnight.
- **dario** (alegre): I promised my aunt I wouldn't be home before dawn. Between us, we keep one whole promise.
- **saul**: When we negotiated the loan, you read every clause. Here you pretend you can't read at all.
- **dario** (molesta): Nobody reads in a ballroom, Captain. They guess. It's more comfortable.
- **saul**: For those who'd rather not be read.
- **narración**: Dario takes a moment to answer. It's the first time anyone has seen him take a moment.
- **dario**: You never talk about yourself either. Only your father, Tiria and duty.
- **saul**: Because that's all there is.
- **dario** (alegre): No. That's all you show. Good night, Captain. Look at your watch less.
- **narración**: Saúl stays on the terrace. For a good while, he doesn't check the time.

> Saúl and Dario talked on the terrace. Neither will tell you what about.

## irene y isolda · A corner full of books, far from the music
*c_irene_isolda · nivel C*

- **narración**: Isolda has had the same page open for quite some time.
- **irene**: You read that page ten minutes ago. I know, because I do the same thing.
- **isolda** (ruborizada): Oh. I… yes. It's just that nobody expects you to talk to anyone here.
- **irene**: I can go.
- **isolda** (ruborizada): No! No. I mean… you don't count. Sorry. That came out terribly.
- **irene** (alegre): It came out honest. I prefer that.
- **narración**: Silence. Isolda grips the book as if it might escape.
- **isolda**: My grandmother's records. You looked for them for three weeks. No one searches three weeks for a stranger.
- **irene**: Lost papers put me in a bad mood. And your grandmother had lovely handwriting.
- **isolda** (ruborizada): That isn't a reason.
- **irene**: No. It isn't.
- **narración**: Neither of them says anything else. Neither of them leaves.

> Irene and Isolda spent the evening in the library. Isolda came out without the book.

## nilo y camila · By the punch bowl
*c_nilo_camila · nivel C*

- **narración**: Camila keeps an eye on Celia from a distance. Nilo keeps an eye on the punch.
- **nilo** (alegre): Mrs. Ferrán. Can I bring you something? A chair, a fan, my cousin back?
- **camila** (alegre): Leave your cousin where she is. It's the first time this week she's laughed without rehearsing it.
- **nilo**: You're a good chaperone.
- **camila**: I'm a tired chaperone. It looks similar, but drinks more punch.
- **nilo**: I heard about the Miravés. About the ship. If there's anything I can do…
- **camila** (molesta): There it is. You offer before you know the price. My husband used to do that too.
- **nilo** (ruborizada): And did it work out for him?
- **camila**: Sometimes. That's why I married him. The other times are why I'm a widow with debts.
- **narración**: Nilo offers nothing more. He pours her some punch, quietly, and stays beside her.
- **camila** (alegre): That, Mr. Lobreda, I'll accept.

> Nilo offered Camila nothing. She says it's the best offer she's had in months.

## teo y celia · A bench at the edge of the dance floor
*c_teo_celia · nivel C*

- **narración**: Celia ties her shoe ribbon. Teo pretends he hasn't been watching her dance.
- **teo** (alegre): Miss Lobreda, you dance as though someone is about to mark you.
- **celia** (molesta): Someone is always marking me, Mr. Rovena. You, for instance.
- **teo**: I don't mark. I admire, rudely.
- **celia**: Why do you never dance?
- **teo**: Because I tread on feet. And when I do, I laugh. And when I laugh, ladies take offence.
- **celia**: Then don't laugh.
- **teo**: It's the only thing I'm good at.
- **narración**: Celia looks at him a moment longer than the etiquette book allows.
- **celia** (alegre): That isn't true. A moment ago you weren't laughing, and you haven't trodden on anyone.
- **teo** (ruborizada): Because I was sitting down.
- **celia** (alegre): Well. One has to start somewhere.

> Teo and Celia shared a bench. He didn't dance. She didn't count her steps.

## dario y celia · As a waltz ends
*c_dario_celia · nivel C*

- **narración**: Dario and Celia are the only ones who haven't missed a single step.
- **dario** (alegre): Miss Lobreda. You're the first partner I haven't had to steer.
- **celia** (alegre): And you're the first who doesn't make me count under my breath.
- **dario**: You count under your breath?
- **celia** (ruborizada): Always. One, two, three. Since I was six. My mother says grace is discipline you can't see.
- **dario**: My aunt says courtesy is a wall you can't see.
- **celia**: And is it true?
- **dario**: It's useful. Which is nearly the same.
- **narración**: Celia considers the answer as if it were a new step.
- **celia**: It doesn't seem the same to me, Mr. Miravés.
- **dario**: No. It isn't. But don't tell my aunt.

> Dario and Celia danced two waltzes in a row. Half the floor stopped to watch.

## teo y irene · By the table of sweets
*c_teo_irene · nivel C*

- **narración**: Teo finishes a joke for a small crowd. Everyone laughs except Irene.
- **teo**: Miss Salvat. My toughest audience. What went wrong? The ending?
- **irene**: The beginning. You told it because someone was about to ask you about the mills.
- **teo** (molesta): What a quick way to ruin a joke.
- **irene**: I didn't ruin it. It worked. Nobody asked.
- **teo**: And are you going to ask?
- **irene**: No. Jokes interest me more than debts. Debts are all the same.
- **teo** (alegre): That's the kindest thing anyone's said to me this autumn.
- **irene** (alegre): It wasn't kind. It was accurate.
- **narración**: Teo laughs. And this time he doesn't tell anyone anything.

> Irene didn't laugh at one of Teo's jokes. He says it's the best thing that happened to him all week.

## saul y isolda · A balcony, away from the ballroom
*c_saul_isolda · nivel C*

- **narración**: Saúl steps out for air. Isolda was already there, and very nearly hides behind the curtain.
- **saul**: Forgive me. I'll go.
- **isolda** (ruborizada): No. It's just… it's a big balcony. There's room.
- **narración**: For a while, neither says a word. It suits them both.
- **saul**: My father wants me to speak with yours. About convoys.
- **isolda**: And mine wants me to speak with you. About the same thing, I think.
- **saul**: You don't seem eager to talk about convoys.
- **isolda**: Nor you about anything.
- **saul** (alegre): That's fair.
- **isolda**: You look at people the way I do. Like someone counting the exits.
- **narración**: Saúl doesn't answer. He offers his arm to go back in, and she takes it without trembling.
- **saul**: It's easier to walk in with someone.
- **isolda**: Yes. For both of us.

> Saúl and Isolda came back from the balcony arm in arm. The ballroom is already talking of convoys and weddings. The two of them are not.

## celia y camila · In the carriage home
*c_celia_camila · nivel C*

- **narración**: Celia goes over the evening under her breath. Camila pretends to be asleep.
- **celia**: I got the third turn wrong.
- **camila**: Nobody saw.
- **celia** (molesta): I saw.
- **camila**: You don't count. You're the only one who always looks at you unkindly.
- **celia**: Have you never made a mistake at a ball?
- **camila** (alegre): At my first ball I trod on a count, knocked over the punch bowl and married the man who laughed.
- **celia**: And was that a mistake?
- **camila**: The punch bowl, yes. The rest I'd do again.
- **narración**: Celia stops counting turns. For the rest of the ride she looks out of the window.

> Camila told Celia how she met her husband. Celia didn't rehearse anything that night.

## dario y isolda · A garden, almost dark
*c_dario_isolda · nivel C*

- **narración**: Dario finds Isolda where he always found her as children: away from everyone.
- **dario** (alegre): Miss Marén. Is there anyone I ought to chase off?
- **isolda** (alegre): We're not eight any more, Mr. Miravés.
- **dario**: You aren't. I'm still eight about most things.
- **isolda**: Nobody laughed at me this time. It's just that… I didn't know what to say.
- **dario**: You used to tell me, and I'd say it out loud for you.
- **isolda**: You can't do that any more.
- **dario**: No. Now you'll have to say it yourself. You'll do it better.
- **narración**: Isolda laughs softly. It's the only sound in the garden.
- **isolda** (ruborizada): And who stands up for you?
- **dario**: No one. But I don't hide in gardens. I hide on the dance floor.

> Dario and Isolda went out to the garden. For once, he came back quieter than she did.

## nilo y celia · A mirrored corridor
*c_nilo_celia · nivel C*

- **narración**: Celia fixes her hair in a mirror. Nilo appears behind her with two glasses.
- **nilo** (alegre): I brought you lemonade. And an excuse, in case you want to leave.
- **celia** (molesta): I don't want to leave. And don't call me “cousin” in front of people.
- **nilo**: There aren't any people here. There's a mirror.
- **celia**: Everyone introduces me as “Nilo's cousin.” As if I were one of your errands.
- **nilo**: I introduce you as Celia. The rest, they add themselves.
- **celia**: Because you get everywhere first. And you help everyone.
- **nilo**: Do you want me to stop helping?
- **celia** (ruborizada): I want you not to help me. Just once, I want to do it on my own.
- **narración**: Nilo sets the lemonade on a table and takes a step back. It costs him more than it looks.
- **nilo** (alegre): Then do it on your own. I'll watch from far away and clap far too loudly.
- **celia** (alegre): That I'll allow.

> Celia asked Nilo not to help her. Nilo is keeping his word, and the effort shows.
